A new Indian startup is targeting a bold challenge in aviation and renewable energy: keeping an aircraft flying continuously for months at a time. **Alteon**, founded by 20-year-old **Ananya Singhal**, has secured backing from notable tech investor **Lachy Groom** to develop its autonomous, wind-energy-harvesting aircraft.

The core concept behind Alteon is to create a persistent aerial platform that can stay aloft for extended durations without traditional fuel. The aircraft are designed to autonomously navigate and exploit different wind currents and jet streams at various altitudes, essentially harvesting energy from the atmosphere to sustain flight. This approach positions the technology somewhere between a satellite and a conventional drone, offering a potentially cheaper and more flexible solution for persistent surveillance, environmental monitoring, communications relaying, and atmospheric research.

The project originates from a deep interest in sustainable technology and aerospace engineering. Singhal, who started working on the concept during his teenage years, emphasizes the goal of creating an always-on, solar-and-wind-powered aerial node. The technical challenges are immense, involving advanced autonomy for dynamic flight path optimization and robust systems designed for prolonged exposure to high-altitude conditions.

The backing from Lachy Groom, a prominent investor known for early bets on infrastructure and deep tech companies, provides crucial early-stage validation and funding. This support will help Alteon accelerate its prototype development and testing as it works to prove the feasibility of its long-endurance flight concept.

If successful, Alteon’s technology could offer a sustainable, cost-effective alternative to orbital satellites for various applications, particularly in regions where ground infrastructure is limited. It represents an innovative convergence of aviation, renewable energy, and autonomous systems, aimed at unlocking a new layer of persistent aerial capability.
