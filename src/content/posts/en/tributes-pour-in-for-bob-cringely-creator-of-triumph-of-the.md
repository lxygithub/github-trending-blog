It is with great sadness that we report the passing of Bob Cringely, a notable figure in the early days of the personal computer revolution. According to reports, Cringely died in his sleep early Saturday morning. Bob, whose real name was Mark Stevens, leaves behind a legacy that bridges the gap between the early days of tech and modern computing history.

Before gaining fame as a writer and documentarian, Cringely served as an early employee at Apple. His tenure at the company placed him at the center of the industry's explosive growth in the late 1970s and early 1980s. However, he is best known to the broader developer and tech community for his influential work with PBS.

His documentary series, particularly the seminal "Triumph of the Nerds," is considered a cornerstone of tech history. The series provided a compelling narrative of how a group of visionaries built the modern computer industry. For many developers, "Triumph of the Nerds" offered a deep dive into the origins of the companies and the personalities that shaped the code we use today. Bob Cringely's unique storytelling will be missed by all who appreciate the history of computing.
